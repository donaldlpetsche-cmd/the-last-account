# Prose and Narrative Standard

## Non-negotiable quality rule

No novel may read as though it was written, drafted, revised, or edited by AI.

Avoid recognizable machine-generated tendencies, including:

- Synthetic symmetry.
- Repetitive paragraph shapes.
- Repeated sentence openings or cadence.
- Overbalanced “not this, but that” constructions.
- Formulaic triads.
- Uniformly polished dialogue.
- Generic emotional labels.
- Summary-heavy scenes.
- Overexplaining motives the scene already demonstrates.
- Repeating the same insight in narration, dialogue, and interior thought.
- Artificially profound closing lines on every scene.
- Constant micro-cliffhangers.
- Excessive fragments used as manufactured intensity.
- Generic competence language without concrete physical or institutional detail.
- Turning Jack into a narrator who constantly announces “patterns.”

Use:

- Natural sentence and paragraph variation.
- Specific sensory and professional detail selected by the viewpoint character.
- Subtext.
- Imperfect, interruptible human dialogue.
- Silence and omission where people would not explain themselves.
- Concrete cause and effect.
- Uneven emotional expression appropriate to character.
- Restraint.

## Viewpoint and tense

- Close third person, past tense.
- One viewpoint per scene.
- Jack is primary viewpoint.
- Secondary viewpoints are limited and purposeful: Avery, Nina, Marcus, Anton, Reese, Hattie, Addison, and Stephen where mapped.
- No head-hopping.
- The narration may not reveal facts the viewpoint character does not know.

## Tone

- Adult.
- Clean and controlled.
- Urbane when the setting warrants it.
- Credible under professional scrutiny.
- Cinematic through geography and consequence, not screenplay shorthand.
- Dry humor grounded in character.
- Emotion carried through behavior, choice, and withheld speech more often than declaration.

## Operations

- Establish geography before action depends on it.
- Show the role of each specialist.
- Make access, surveillance, communications, timing, and exit constraints legible.
- Plant fair clues.
- Let plans fail for specific causal reasons.
- Avoid magic hacking, impossible physical recovery, and unexplained access.
- Injuries persist.
- The crew succeeds collectively, not because everyone else becomes incompetent.

## Violence

- Violence is dangerous, fast, and consequential.
- Jack and the crew prefer control, misdirection, escape, and disabling force.
- Killing is last-resort protection of life and carries emotional and legal consequences.

## Chapter endings

End on a changed fact, decision, danger, cost, or relationship state. Do not force an aphorism or generic teaser when the scene's concrete consequence is stronger.
