# Jack Mallory Series Bible

## Franchise definition

**Working series name:** *Jack Mallory Series*  
**Long-range goal:** 30+ commercial thriller novels  
**Season One:** 10 books  
**Target length:** 45,000–60,000 words per book  
**Book structure:** One apparently impossible, high-stakes operation per novel  
**Publishing intent:** Rapid-release friendly; ebook and audiobook viable; adaptation-aware without writing like a screenplay

## Core reader promise

Impossible missions carried out by people institutions consider criminals, for reasons the reader urgently wants to succeed.

The crew's objectives are usually morally defensible. Their methods may include deception, intrusion, theft, manipulation, social engineering, unauthorized access, and other illegal acts. Suspense must ask both:

- Can they do it?
- Should they do it?

Every novel must provide:

- A complete standalone operation and satisfying external resolution.
- A meaningful advance in the Season One threat.
- A crew or character consequence that persists.
- The pleasure of expertise, planning, improvisation, and elegant execution.
- A point where the plan fails or becomes materially more dangerous.
- An ending that leaves the reader exhilarated, satisfied, and emotionally invested.

## Creative DNA

The series draws structural strengths, not plots or imitation, from:

- **Leverage:** team-based impossible operations, reversals, moral satisfaction.
- **White Collar:** intelligence, charm, culture, elite access, confidence over brute force.
- **Heat:** professional danger, consequence, pursuit, and physical escalation.
- **Mission: Impossible:** planning, specialized roles, discipline, and escalating complications.
- **Ocean's Eleven:** ensemble chemistry, misdirection, and the pleasure of watching a plan work.

The result must be original in plot, character, voice, and surface execution.

## Emotional engine

The permanent crew is Jack's found family. The series explores:

- Loyalty without sentimentality.
- The cost of living outside institutions.
- Protection of people abandoned by legitimate systems.
- Redemption through action rather than speeches.
- The tension between competence and control.
- The damage caused when Jack protects people by withholding choices from them.
- The difference between being useful to a family and being emotionally present in it.

## The crew

The permanent core is:

- **Jack Mallory — Architect:** objective definition, planning, social engineering, legal and institutional reading, negotiation, cover design, overall command.
- **Nina Rodriquez — Systems:** surveillance, networks, communications, identities, electronic access, intelligence synthesis.
- **Marcus Lowell — Security:** counter-surveillance, logistics, tactical planning, transport, protection, extraction, threat assessment.
- **Anton Rominov — Entry:** locks, vaults, mechanical systems, restoration hardware, precision access.
- **Reese Long — Access:** reconnaissance, climbing, confined movement, physical infiltration, improvisation.

Rotating specialists may be used, but the permanent five remain the franchise center.

## Crew governance

- Jack leads the mission as a whole.
- Each specialist controls decisions inside that specialist's domain.
- Any member may reject a job before it begins.
- The crew does not abandon one another.
- The crew is already financially secure. Money may be accepted, but greed is not the primary engine.
- They take cases for challenge, purpose, loyalty, justice, or because formal systems failed.
- Clients and objectives are vetted.

## Moral boundaries

The crew normally will not:

- Kill.
- Torture.
- Kidnap.
- Harm innocents.
- Exploit children.
- Steal from ordinary people.
- Betray a client after accepting a properly vetted mission.
- Create unnecessary danger for police or uninvolved responders.

Lethal force is permissible only to protect life when no reasonable alternative remains. It must be reluctant, consequential, and emotionally costly. Casual killing is incompatible with the franchise.

## Victory types

Victories should vary:

- Clean and elegant.
- Costly but necessary.
- Morally complicated.
- Publicly misunderstood.
- Legally incomplete but practically decisive.
- Externally successful while creating an internal fracture.

## Geographic baseline

Jack is Boston-born and Book One is Boston-centered. The series can become nationally and globally mobile after establishing the crew, their family life, and their Boston operating ecosystem.

## Series-scale arcs currently established

- Hattie Baker gradually builds an evidence-based case around Jack's proximity to improbable outcomes.
- Addison Moody and AM Strategic Advisory form the hidden Season One adversarial structure.
- The reader learns Addison's position before Jack does.
- Jack and Addison develop genuine attraction while standing on opposing sides and concealing central truths.
- Matthew “Matty” Mallory, Jack's estranged brother, remains a future emotional and operational collision.
- Anton's unfinished intelligence history may eventually connect to Hattie's investigation.

Exact Book Two through Book Ten plots remain unresolved.
