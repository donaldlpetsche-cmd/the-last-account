# The Last Account — Injury and Relationship Ledger

## Reese injury chain

1. Preexisting severe ankle history from gymnastics career.
2. Chapter 10: brief ankle failure during test climb; Reese hides it.
3. Chapter 18: pain increases in shaft; Reese reports normal.
4. Chapter 22: ankle fails while crossing unsupported gap; she falls, sustains serious leg injury, becomes trapped, and continues holding bypass.
5. Chapter 26: Marcus frees and carries Reese during extraction.
6. Chapter 27: Reese undergoes surgery.
7. Chapter 28 and future books: injury persists. No instantaneous return to baseline.

## Jack and Marcus

### Before operation
Longstanding command trust. Marcus accepts Jack's overall leadership while controlling security and abort thresholds.

### Fracture
Jack withholds mission-critical information about targeted witnesses. Marcus recommends abort based on available risk. Jack refuses without providing the information that would allow informed specialist judgment.

### End state
Marcus completes the rescue and remains family, but does not offer immediate forgiveness. Jack must rebuild trust through future conduct.

## Jack and Reese

Younger-sibling dynamic. Reese wants autonomy and fears being managed or replaced. Jack's withholding combines with her own concealment of injury, producing a failure neither can assign wholly to the other.

## Crew family

The Lowell home and Sunday dinner establish that spouses and children are part of the crew's real life but not operational resources. Operational decisions therefore threaten an existing family structure, not an abstract team.

## Jack and Hattie

Professional suspicion begins to sharpen. Hattie does not know the crew but recognizes Jack's proximity to suppressed records and impossible outcomes.

## Jack and Addison

No informed relationship exists in Book One. Addison sees Jack as the leading explanation for her failed operation. Jack does not know she is the hidden principal. Attraction is a future beat, not retroactive Book One knowledge.
